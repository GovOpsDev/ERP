# Budget control design — experimental, not frozen

Upstream has two independent checks: analytic control optionally narrowed by account-to-KPI mapping, and allocation by analytic account, fund and dynamic analytic dimension values. Company and OU are additional isolation boundaries in the target.

The provisional `BudgetControlKey` is company, period, OU, primary Odoo analytic account, expense account, fund and a set of Odoo analytic accounts in distinct root plans. `_resolve` maps the account to a KPI through the period template when present and resolves exactly one open analytic/KPI control and one fund/dimension control. Both must pass. A no-KPI analytic control is supported, but an ambiguous combination fails closed. Analytic distribution percentages and multi-combination allocation are not yet implemented: callers must not treat the current key as a complete Odoo analytic-distribution contract.

`erp.budget.control` stores draft/open/closed allocation limits. `erp.budget.move` stores signed commitment entries and reversal references, unique per control and origin. `_check_key` is check-only and `_commit_key` resolves then commits to both levels; `_reverse` retains the original entry and adds an idempotent opposite entry. Available per level is its allocation less the sum of ledger moves. Origins are currently opaque strings, not yet validated PR/PO document references; the service is private and not a stable integration API.

Every commit locks all affected control rows in ascending ID order, updates a lock version, then calculates ledger sums before writing. The version update is necessary under repeatable-read isolation so concurrent transactions with old snapshots fail serialization. Odoo normally retries serialization failures at its transaction boundary; callers must not suppress errors or commit partial work. The check-only operation locks but does not persist a move. Organization and company rules and capability checks apply independently, including when a caller passes a recordset created with elevated access.

This is a partial, experimental implementation. It lacks PR and PO lifecycle/reconciliation, document-linked provenance and state, multi-line atomic batching, allocation/plan lifecycle parity, uniqueness of equivalent allocation definitions, finance actuals, historic import, full Odoo analytic-distribution support, and comprehensive cross-company/dimensional and integration tests. Do not use it as a production budget control or mark Budget MIGRATED. The Budget Lifecycle v1 document remains draft.

## Provisional purchase reconciliation

`_reconcile_purchase` accepts a PR-line family key, PR eligibility/amount and the current list of confirmed PO-line keys/amounts, all for the **same pair of control allocations**. It locks both controls, computes the target state, verifies both ledger levels agree, checks the final total at both levels, then adds signed typed reversal/commitment records in a savepoint. If at least one PO is confirmed it removes the full PR-line commitment; when the final PO disappears and the PR remains eligible it restores the PR amount. Repeated identical input does not duplicate ledger entries. Source identity and state are supplied by the caller and are not independently verified by this provisional helper; it must not be exposed to RPC or used for real documents until the Procurement adapter validates source authorization and states. POs with different budget keys, multi-line atomic approval, a closed control or expired period, currency/tax conversion, actuals and persisted source references are not supported.

`_reconcile_purchase_multi` additionally supports PR and PO lines on distinct control pairs. It checks source authorization in the Procurement adapter, collects authorized historical and target controls, locks all affected rows in sorted order, verifies paired ledger history, and atomically replaces PR and PO commitments at their own keys. The Procurement caller pre-locks all affected controls in sorted order across lines. Multi-distribution percentages, taxed/foreign-currency PO lines, closed-period cancellation, full OCA tier parity, accounting actuals and broader upgrade coverage remain incomplete.

## Shared plan ceiling — tested subset

An optional confirmed `erp.budget.plan` per company period has unique lines per analytic account. The line's OU is an authorization owner, not a financial partition: all analytic-level controls for the same company/period/analytic contribute, across KPI, fund, dimensions and OUs. Consumption is the signed sum of immutable `erp.budget.move` rows on analytic-level controls only; fund-level duplicate ledger rows are not added again. The cap is initial amount plus immutable approved increases and incoming transfers minus decreases and outgoing transfers. Parent available = cap - analytic ledger sum; every child control still checks its own amount. Reconciliation checks all parent deltas together, so PR-to-PO and commitment-to-actual replacement does not double count, and reversals follow the existing ledger.

`limits` mode means child control amounts are independent maximums, not allocated shares. `allocated` mode treats open/closed active analytic controls as allocations and prohibits their sum from exceeding the parent, including on opening and reduction. This is a target subset: OCA's exact equality and full dated KPI planning are not implemented.

All budget authorization paths and plan activation/changes update a sorted period row lock/version, in addition to existing sorted child row locks. The period lock prevents configuration races and stale repeatable-read snapshots across different child keys, at the cost of serializing activity within a period. This is not load-tested. A period with no plan retains legacy controls; confirming a plan activates enforcement for the entire period and requires every existing analytic control to map to a ceiling. No caps are inferred from legacy data. A confirmed plan cannot be disabled; new analytic scope requires further lifecycle work.

Manager-approved increases/decreases and same-company/same-period transfers create immutable events. Decrease and transfer reject a negative ceiling or a ceiling below net ledger consumption, and allocated mode also checks child allocations. This is a redesigned event history, not the upstream `base.revision` document/version chain or tiered/reversible transfer document. Historical backfill and production migration require explicit source plans, allocation, revisions and ledger reconciliation.

A manager confirming or adjusting a shared plan must have read access to every analytic child control in scope; this prevents changing or exposing an aggregate covering an OU outside the manager’s assignment. Operational posting can consume a shared parent without giving a child OU user access to other OU records.

## Version, KPI release and transfer reversal — PARTIAL extension

`erp.budget.plan.version` stores numbered full snapshots. An explicit prepare action creates a labelled baseline of a pre-existing effective plan without inventing historic reviewers, then creates a draft. One partial unique index permits at most one effective version per plan. Activation under the period lock validates the base version, current cap, commitments/actuals and allocated children, then atomically creates linked delta events and supersedes the prior snapshot. Existing non-versioned adjustments remain effective; current capacity is still derived from the original line and all signed events. The snapshot records capacity at version activation, not an assertion that later approved transfers never change it.

Optional `release_required` on an allocated plan line makes `erp.budget.kpi.release` the gate for opening analytic KPI controls. Draft release has no effect; its declared amount must equal the child amounts and the aggregate must fit the parent. Ordered reviewers are configured by Settings administrators for company/OU. Submitter and reviewers must differ; reviewers must be Budget managers with active company/OU access. Rejections preserve review rounds; a reset can be resubmitted. Final approval and opening child controls are atomic. In periods explicitly set to `formal` by a Settings administrator, every line must require a formal release; compatibility periods default to `legacy`. This is explicit opt-in, not inferred historical approval.

A released control cannot be edited or closed independently. Reversal is allowed when all affected child controls have zero net consumption; it closes the controls, records the actor/time and frees their allocation without deleting ledger history. Reversal is idempotent. Plan transfer reversal similarly creates a unique linked inverse event, checks destination consumption/allocations, and is idempotent. These are tested target redesigns. Full source allocation dimensions and revisions, exact OCA tier definitions, tier approval of plan revisions and transfers, source control versions and source transfer-document submit/reverse lifecycle remain required migration work.

## Snapshot event boundary — compatibility subset

Newly created baseline snapshots and activated plan versions store the maximum plan-event ID for their period while holding the existing period lock. `_ceiling()` resolves their snapshot line amount plus effects of subsequent events, excluding events already represented by the snapshot. Missing lines in an anchored effective snapshot fail closed. Revision activation flushes the superseded state before activating its successor so the one-effective-version index remains valid even on repeated revisions. Subsequent manager adjustment and transfer events remain economically active implementation effects; this is not yet the requested source allocation/control/transfer authority.

Previously stored effective versions have no provable event boundary and retain the existing initial amount plus events resolver. Their current cap is not guessed or rewritten. An explicit later activation creates a new snapshot with a recorded boundary. This compatibility path still needs a deliberate migration policy before claiming all effective caps resolve exclusively from versions. No reviewer or approval provenance is inferred.

## Initial source allocation subset

For a period with no plan or controls, a completed allocation creates a protected draft plan, aggregating its amounts by analytic, and one protected draft fund control per organization/analytic/fund line. The line's released amount is copied from its allocated amount at completion. Opening either control level is forbidden until the generated plan is confirmed, and opening an unbacked fund control in this period is forbidden. Existing periods are not auto-converted. Completed documents cannot be cancelled; direct plan adjustments are denied and supported revisions must go through the allocation revision action described below. Analytic/KPI controls remain independently limited under the target's legacy or formal KPI policy. This is not full source allocation/control revision parity.

## Allocation revision subset

For an initial completed allocation with a confirmed plan, a manager can prepare a numbered immutable-history successor of the existing fund/analytic lines. The previous effective snapshot remains in force while drafts exist. Activation checks access to all OUs and controls, serialized period/controls, unchanged fund base amounts, each fund's net ledger consumption, shared parent consumption and (in allocated mode) existing KPI allocations. It creates the linked effective plan snapshot and updates existing fund control amounts in one transaction without rewriting ledger moves. The allocation's original line amounts remain the initial historical values; after a revision, its effective revision snapshot is the authority for current fund amounts, checked against the operational controls before another revision. Competing drafts become stale when one succeeds.

This supports amount changes on existing dimensions only. It does not create upstream control-version records, support allocation-line additions/removals, revise released KPI controls, or implement OCA tier validation; completed allocation cancellation remains unsupported. It is therefore a PARTIAL adaptation, not full source parity.

On source-allocation-backed formal periods, KPI release submission and final release require the combined analytic KPI controls to equal the current parent released cap. Changing that cap through allocation revision is rejected while previously released KPI controls have a nonzero different total; coordinated KPI revision is not yet implemented. Legacy/independent periods retain their earlier target behavior. This aggregate check does not create upstream date-range planning matrix rows or enforce its initial-balance check.

Existing allocation dimensions may be reduced to zero through a revision if consumption and other parent/KPI constraints allow it. This economically disables that fund control while preserving its identity and ledger, and may be restored by a later revision. It is not a full implementation of adding/removing source dimension rows.

Upgrade does not rewrite or automatically close formal KPI releases that were already done under the prior less-than-or-equal rule. The new equality check applies to subsequent submissions/releases and relevant revisions. Such existing source-backed partial releases require explicit reconciliation before claiming source parity; historical approvals are not fabricated.

Allocation revision now supports adding fund/analytic/additional dimensions and explicit exclusion with zero net consumption. Pending dimensions have no control or capacity until atomic activation; new analytics enter the linked plan snapshot with base zero, without modifying predecessor snapshots. Excluded identities and all ledger history remain. This is a tested dimension-revision adaptation, not completion of source control/KPI versions or tier/carry-forward workflows.

Opening Reconciliation records source initial available plus initial commitment as a planning minimum, with company/OU access and stable origin reference. Confirmation, revision activation, decreases, transfer reversal and formal KPI release enforce that minimum. It never changes parent capacity or creates ledger consumption. Real carried commitments require their separate provenance-preserving ledger workflow.

The effective planning/control version is a KPI release with immutable amount/date snapshots, predecessor/root/version identity and a plan-version link. Prepare Planning Revision can select a draft allocation revision. Ordered reviewers approve the proposed planning; allocation-linked planning then waits in Approved state until the allocation action atomically activates the plan, fund and KPI amounts. The preceding control version remains effective throughout review. Current control rows are the operational projection of these approved versions and retain immutable ledger identity. New plan confirmation records a baseline at the real confirmation time; pre-existing target history is retained as compatibility state until an explicit successor is reviewed. Planning dates do not filter transaction-date availability. Source tier adapters cover control and transfer, not an additional plan/allocation engine.

Completed allocation Cancel prepares an explicit zero-capacity revision after zero net consumption is verified for every control. Formal child planning requires ordered review before activation closes previously open controls and marks the allocation cancelled. Reset/Restore creates a new successor and keeps the cancellation effective until activation; restoration uses the last effective dimensions, preserving earlier exclusions and every old snapshot/ledger ID. Direct plan edits/increases and new control opening remain blocked for cancelled allocation-backed plans. Scope controls are locked in sorted order before the period lock, preventing an inverse analytic/fund lock order in coordinated revisions.

Activity-configured templates select the KPI by activity rather than account alone. Activity binds the budget account; posted source documents retain that key even when the native invoice GL account differs. Activity is optional for legacy account-only templates. Source adapters pass it to Budget APIs; activity itself never grants additional capacity. Effective activity mappings and used accounts are protected, source document fields become immutable with commitment/posting, and ordered activity locking precedes control/period locking in multiline workflows.
